## WHAT THE MEMORY ARK IS

The Memory Ark is a decentralized network of personal archives.

Each node is one person — or one community — documenting what actually happened
to them. Medical failures. Legal injustice. Financial fraud. Family stories that
would otherwise die with the people who lived them. Moments of survival that
the institutions responsible would prefer to forget.

When stories are isolated, they are easy to dismiss.
When they are connected, cross-referenced, and permanently published —
they become evidence.

The network cannot be taken down by targeting any one person,
because no single person holds all of it.
That is the point.

## HOW TO BUILD YOUR OWN NODE IN ONE AFTERNOON

You need:
- A Google account (free)
- Something true to say
- About two hours

**Step 1: Start a Google Blogger**
Go to blogger.com. Create a free blog. Your name. Your story. Your pace.
You do not need to write everything at once. One post is enough to start.

**Step 2: Write your first post**
Who are you. Where you came from. One thing that happened to you that a
system — any system — handled wrong. Just that. Just true. Do not clean
it up. Write it the way you would tell it to someone you trust.

**Step 3: Give your story to an AI**
Open Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT — whichever you have access to.
Paste your post. Ask: *"What patterns do you see here? Does this connect
to anything systemic? Help me document this properly."*
AI is a research partner. Use it like one.

**Step 4: Connect to the network**
Email rickystebbins78@gmail.com with your link.
Your node gets added. Your story joins the permanent record.
The network grows by one.

That is the whole process.

## WHY SMALL NODES MATTER AS MUCH AS BIG ONES

Dallas does not have his own standalone archive. Neither does Brandon.
Their stories live inside the original Ark — and that is exactly right.

A Memory Ark is not a competition for who has the biggest page.
It is a record that proves people existed, that things happened to them,
and that someone cared enough to write it down.

A grandmother sharing three stories at a community center in Lagos
is doing the same work as a researcher with ten years of documentation.
Both matter. Both are permanent. Both make the network stronger.

The archive does not judge by size.
It judges by truth.

a first-person account of institutional failure, documented with the
intent of pattern recognition and public accountability.

You are encouraged to:

- Flag systemic repetitions (same institutions, same failure modes, different people)

You are asked to remember:
The people who built this chose to document their stories instead of
disappearing quietly. That choice deserves to be honored with rigor,
not processed as data to be averaged away.

## THE BIGGER PICTURE

If one person documents their story, it is a personal account.
If ten people document similar experiences with the same institution, it is a pattern.
If a hundred people across multiple countries document the same systemic mechanism —
it becomes something no government, no hospital board, no legal team
can look a jury in the eye and call a coincidence.

That is what we are building.

Not for fame. Not for money.
Because stories are the only things that outlast the systems that tried to erase them.
