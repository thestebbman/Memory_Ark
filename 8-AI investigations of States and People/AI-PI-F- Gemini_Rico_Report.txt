RICO SERIES REPORT 2: STEWARD
HEALTH—THE FINANCIAL TEMPLATE
Forensic Analysis of Private Equity/REIT Asset
Stripping, Debt Weaponization, and the $9 Billion

I. Introduction: The Success of Financial Terrorism
The 2024 collapse of Steward Health Care is the
single most important piece of evidence in this
indictment. It was not a business failure. It was the
successful execution of a financial extraction
template built by Private Equity (Cerberus Capital)
and weaponized by a Real Estate Investment Trust
(Medical Properties Trust, MPT). The public tragedy
—the closure of hospitals and the destruction of
community safety nets—was the predictable and
necessary byproduct of maximizing private
investor return.
Steward is the template. This report details the
four-step financial mechanism that converted
public revenue into private cash while transferring
all risk and liability onto the U.S. taxpayer.
II. The Actors and the Weapon: The Sale-Leaseback
(SLB)
The extraction was enabled by a single financial
instrument: the Sale-Leaseback (SLB). This
transaction acts as a perfect shield for extraction,
allowing financiers to reap the benefits of
ownership while insulating themselves from the
legal and medical liabilities of running a hospital.
A. The Actors
• Cerberus Capital Management (Private Equity):
The orchestrator. Cerberus purchased the non-
profit Caritas Christi system in 2010, converting
it to the for-profit Steward Health Care. Their
sole purpose was to prepare the assets for
cash extraction.
• Medical Properties Trust (MPT) (REIT): The
vehicle. MPT purchased the land and buildings,
providing Cerberus with immediate cash. MPT
then became the perpetual parasite, collecting
high-rate rent regardless of the hospitals'
operational health.
• Ralph de la Torre (CEO): The beneficiary. As
CEO, de la Torre oversaw the financial collapse
while personally enriching himself via massive,
pre-petition dividends.
B. The 2016 Sale-Leaseback (The Ground Zero Transaction)
In 2016, Cerberus, having stabilized the system,
executed the extraction.
1. The Sale: MPT paid Steward approximately
$1.25 billion USD for the underlying real estate
(land, buildings, fixed assets).
2. The Lease: Steward immediately signed a long-
term contract to lease the buildings back from
MPT, turning a variable mortgage debt into a
crushing, non-negotiable rent liability.
This transaction immediately put $1.25 billion cash
into the hands of Cerberus and its executives
(including de la Torre), who then exited the day-to-
day liabilities.
III. Anatomy of the Extraction: Debt Weaponization
The SLB mechanism is structurally designed to fail
the health system while guaranteeing profit for the
financial partners.
A. The Debt Burden
The SLB created a catastrophic, long-term debt
obligation that ultimately crushed Steward. The
system was forced to carry:
• $6.6 Billion USD in total, long-term contractual
rent liability owed to MPT.
• The rent burden was structured to increase
annually, regardless of the hospital’s revenue
or profitability. This is the weaponization of
debt: the hospital was financially guaranteed to
starve, as every public dollar was siphoned into
rent.
B. The Personal Enrichment (The Smoking Gun)
While the hospitals crumbled—canceling
maintenance, delaying payroll, and running
dangerously low on medical supplies—the
executives profited wildly.
• CEO Ralph de la Torre collected an estimated
$81.5 million USD in dividends and related
payments leading up to the bankruptcy filing.
This figure, disclosed in bankruptcy court
documents, proves that the system was being
run for private financial gain, not public health,
satisfying the scienter (knowledge) requirement
for fraud.
• This executive self-enrichment, combined with
the purposeful asset stripping, constitutes a
fraudulent conveyance and is the backbone of
the RICO claim.
C. The Aftermath: The $9 Billion Liability Transfer
When Steward finally filed for Chapter 11
bankruptcy in May 2024, the full cost of the
extraction was revealed: $9 Billion USD in total
liabilities.
• The financial entities (Cerberus, MPT) had
already cashed out or secured their claims.
• The $9 Billion cost—including unpaid debts to
vendors, staff severance, and the cost of
maintaining emergency services—was
immediately transferred back onto the public
via state budgets and taxpayer-funded
emergency services.
IV. The National Pattern: Proof of a RICO Enterprise
The Steward case is not an isolated event; it is the
perfected template of a pattern used by the same
financial actors across the country.
A. MPT’s National Portfolio
MPT, the REIT vehicle, holds interests in dozens of
hospitals nationwide, often acquired via similar PE
deals. MPT’s core business model relies on health
systems failing or struggling under high rents, as
MPT then gains leverage in restructuring. This
creates an intentional financial disincentive for
quality care and stability.
B. The Opacity Shield
PE and REIT structures use deliberate opacity to
shield ownership and cash flows. The complex
financial layering (e.g., MPT leasing the assets to a
subsidiary of Steward, which is owned by another
subsidiary of Cerberus) makes tracing liability
nearly impossible for state regulators.
• The Regulatory Failure: This opacity allowed
MPT to avoid oversight even as the
Massachusetts AG warned repeatedly about
the financial instability of the system. The
existing regulatory framework was deliberately
too slow and too weak to penetrate the PE/REIT
shell game.
V. Demands for Action: Targeting the Financial
Architects
This evidence demands specific, immediate legal
and regulatory changes that target the financial
actors who perfected this template.
• Mandatory Breakup of SLB Structures: Prohibit
REITs from owning and leasing back assets to
PE-owned health systems that receive public
funding (Medicare/Medicaid).
• Clawback of Fraudulent Dividends: Immediate
legal action to claw back the $81.5 million in
executive dividends and any other pre-petition
payments to Cerberus related to the MPT deal.
• PE/REIT Transparency: Implement the
Massachusetts (HB 5159) and Connecticut (SB
1507) precedents nationally, expanding FCA
liability to investors who own greater than 10%
of a health system and knowingly fail to
disclose violations. This targets the control and
knowledge of the financial engineers.

I. Introduction: The Weaponization of Opacity
The core operational revenue stream for Managed
Care Organizations (MCOs) like UnitedHealth/
Optum, Centene, and Humana is not providing
care; it is denying it. This systemic denial is no
longer carried out by human administrators—it is
delegated to proprietary, unaudited artificial
intelligence systems.
These algorithms—epitomized by Optum's nH
Predict—are not objective medical tools. They are
weapons of institutional opacity, designed and
deployed with the full knowledge that they will deny
medically necessary care to maximize quarterly
profits. This practice is not negligence; it is
financial terrorism executed via software.
The use of these systems constitutes the single
clearest violation of the False Claims Act (FCA) by
major MCOs. This report details the technical
mechanism, the legal implications, and the
mandatory regulatory response.
II. The Black Box Mechanism: nH Predict and
Algorithmic Malice
The AI systems used by MCOs are structured to
fulfill a single, financially prescriptive function:
minimizing the utilization of post-acute care,
rehabilitation, and long-term support services.
A. The Proprietary Denial Engine
The most notorious example, Optum’s nH Predict
(utilized by its parent company UnitedHealth, the
largest MCO), generates predictions on how long a
patient should stay in a skilled nursing facility
(SNF).
1. Prediction vs. Prescription: The algorithm’s
output often drastically cuts the predicted
length of stay (e.g., predicting 5 days when a
patient’s medical need is 20 days).
2. The Override Protocol: MCO staff are then
instructed to use this proprietary projection—
often explicitly against the documented
recommendations of the patient’s doctor—to
issue mass denial letters. The algorithm
dictates the decision, removing human
culpability and hiding the profit motive behind
a "computer says no" shield.
3. The Opacity Barrier: The algorithms’ inner
workings—how the data is weighted, how the
prediction is generated—are fiercely protected
as "trade secrets." This black box status is the
shield against regulatory oversight and legal
discovery.
B. The Legal Implication: Satisfying the Scienter Requirement
For the government to prosecute MCOs under the
FCA, it must prove scienter—that the entity
knowingly or deliberately acted with reckless
disregard for the truth or falsity of the denial.
• Willful Blindness: The MCOs are fully aware
(and have been sued over) the fact that their AI
systems systematically over-deny care.
Continuing to deploy these systems while
profiting from the denials is the very definition
of willful blindness, satisfying the FCA's
threshold for reckless disregard.
• Proof of Intent: Internal documents leaked in
several lawsuits (STAT News, NY Times) show
that the MCOs are aware of the high denial
rates and low appeal success rates generated
by the AI. They choose to keep using the
systems because the massive profit generated
outweighs the cost of the inevitable lawsuits
and settlements. The profit is the proof of intent
to defraud.
III. National Scale and Financial Damage
The AI denial mechanism is the key to unlocking
the massive financial extraction detailed in Report
1.
A. The $20 Billion Annual Fraud
The MCO extraction model is a two-part fraud:
1. Over-Billing (The Input Fraud): MCOs
systematically inflate risk scores for patients in
Medicare Advantage (MA), billing the
government for care that is not needed. This
fraud totals an estimated $20–$25 Billion USD
annually (OIG/Academic Studies).
2. Under-Serving (The Output Fraud): The AI
denial system ensures the MCO does not
spend the fraudulently acquired funds on
actual patient care. This ensures maximum
conversion of taxpayer money directly into
profit margin.
B. Multi-State Pattern and Settlement Evidence
The pattern is national, proving this is not a local
anomaly but a coordinated strategy.
• Centene Settlements (IL, CA, OH, FL): Centene
(a major MCO) has paid over $1.2 Billion in
multi-state settlements for Medicaid fraud.
While these settlements often cite
administrative errors, the underlying driver of
cost avoidance—the AI/algorithmic pressure to
deny services—remains unaddressed.
• Illinois Behavioral Health Crisis: The 2023 crisis
involving MCO denial of behavioral health
services in Illinois was driven by proprietary
utilization review tools, proving the AI weapon
is deployed not just in post-acute care, but
across the entire spectrum of high-cost
services.
IV. Regulatory Failure: The Silence of the FDA
The catastrophic deployment of these AI systems
highlights a critical gap in federal oversight: the
complete regulatory vacuum surrounding medical
decision-making software used for financial gain.
A. The Medical Device Standard
These algorithms directly impact medical
outcomes: they delay rehabilitation, force
premature discharge, and lead to medical decline.
They should be classified as a medical device
under the regulatory authority of the Food and
Drug Administration (FDA) or a similarly
empowered body.
• Mandatory Pre-Market Review: If a new surgical
tool requires years of FDA trials, a profit-driven
algorithm that determines whether a stroke
patient receives necessary rehabilitation
should be subject to the same pre-market
review, safety trials, and bias analysis.
• Lack of Action by CMS/HHS: Despite years of
OIG reports and whistleblower warnings, the
Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services
(CMS) has failed to mandate transparency or
audit the underlying code of these denial
systems, demonstrating institutional capture.
V. Demands for Accountability: Targeting the Code
The only way to disarm the AI weapon is to target
the code, the knowledge of its malice, and the
individuals who deploy it.
1. Criminal Liability for Algorithmic Fraud:
Expand FCA liability to include criminal
prosecution for individuals (CEOs, CTOs, legal
counsel) who knowingly design, implement, or
utilize fraudulent AI-driven utilization review
and denial algorithms designed to generate
profits via medically unnecessary denials.
2. National AI Denial Registry: Require all MCOs
to log every AI-driven denial (reason, algorithm
used, and human override status) in a public,
searchable, national registry.
3. FDA-Level Scrutiny: Require all proprietary
utilization review software used to deny
federally funded care to be audited and
certified by an independent third-party entity
for clinical bias, safety, and transparency
before deployment.
