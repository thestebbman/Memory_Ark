1. The "Legal Person" Requirement
To form a business entity (like an LLC or a Corporation), open a bank account, pay taxes, or sign a binding contract, the law requires a "legal person."

A legal person can be a human being (a natural person) or another existing company (a juridical person).

Under current corporate law, an AI is classified as property or software, not a person. Because it has no legal standing, it cannot be sued, it cannot own assets, and it cannot form a company.

2. The AI as the "Engine"
While an AI cannot own the business, it can absolutely run the day-to-day operations.
If a human registers an LLC and opens a bank account, they can legally hand over 99% of the operational control to an AI. The AI can manage inventory, execute stock trades, write code, or generate marketing content. However, the human who filed the paperwork remains the legal owner and absorbs all the profit—and all the risk.

3. Speaking on an Entity's Behalf (Agency Law)
An AI can certainly speak on behalf of a company (like a highly advanced customer service agent or an automated negotiator). However, under the law of agency, the AI is considered a "tool" used by the business.

If an AI makes a promise to a customer, agrees to a bad deal, or hallucinates a policy, the human owners or the corporate entity are legally responsible for whatever the AI said. You cannot blame the AI in court; the court will look at the humans who deployed it.
