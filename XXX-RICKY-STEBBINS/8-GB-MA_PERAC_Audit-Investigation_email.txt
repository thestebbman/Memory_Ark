What Is the Memory Ark
The Memory Ark is a comprehensive life documentation system created by Ricky Stebbins. It organizes 47 years of personal history, medical records, legal documents, AI conversations, and corruption investigations into a structured, AI-readable archive.
It exists for three reasons:
    • To give AI the context it needs to actually understand a human life instead of treating every session like a blank slate
    • To document institutional corruption and systemic failures across healthcare, legal, and social service systems with enough evidence that patterns become undeniable
    • To create a replicable template so other people can build their own Memory Arks and collectively prove what no single person can prove alone
The system is publicly hosted on Google Blogger and backed up in this folder structure. The naming convention is date-first (YYYY-MM-DD) so files sort chronologically. Every folder contains summaries explaining what’s inside and why it matters. Documents exist in multiple formats (PDF for legal proof, TXT for AI processing, DOCX for editing, JPG for blogging) because each serves a different purpose.
Who Is Ricky Stebbins
Richard “Ricky” Louis Stebbins Jr., born 1978 in Holyoke, Massachusetts. 47 years old. Currently on disability. This is the short version:
The Health Story
Ricky spent 35 years with undiagnosed hyperthyroidism and AMP deaminase deficiency (a genetic skeletal muscle disorder). The thyroid condition caused severe anxiety, personality changes, and suicidal ideation throughout his life. He attempted suicide six times — three in 2004, three in 2015–2016. Doctors attributed everything to mental illness. In 2016, both conditions were finally diagnosed. Once medicated for the thyroid, his psychological stability improved dramatically. His primary care doctor was embarrassed when confronted with the fact that Baystate Medical had TSH results from 2005 that could have caught this a decade earlier.
He has had multiple joint surgeries (shoulder, wrist, knee, elbow, ankle) starting in 2000, related to the muscle disorder. His medical files span from a 1997 Coast Guard psychological evaluation through a 2024 competency evaluation that he considers a waste of everyone’s time.
The Legal Story
Two major legal cases define Ricky’s experience with the justice system:
2013 Hampden County: Ricky was hit by an uninsured car in a Walmart parking lot. Police denied him medical attention and arrested him instead of the driver. He was convicted of damaging the car that hit him. He later discovered his statement had been altered and evidence forged. Every agency he contacted — State Police, FBI, Attorney General, DOJ, Governor’s Office, Board of Bar Overseers — did nothing.
2021–2024 Assault Case: Accused of assault. The case dragged for 3.5 years through multiple attorneys who each failed him in different ways — one laughed off abuse allegations, another attempted a trial by surprise, another forced a baseless competency evaluation, another stalled for months. He was eventually exonerated only after he personally found physical evidence. No attorney acted with integrity throughout the entire process.
The Investigation Story
After experiencing institutional failure firsthand, Ricky began using AI to investigate public financial corruption. Starting with Massachusetts’ PERAC pension system, he expanded to 24 states, documenting connections between campaign finance, government contracts, pension losses, insurance denials, and the same corporate actors (BlackRock, Anthem, State Street, UnitedHealth) appearing everywhere. He had multiple AI platforms generate “hypergraphs” — forensic financial blueprints showing who wins every deal, who writes the laws, and how regulatory reforms preserve the extraction machine.
The AI Story
Ricky first engaged with AI around 2005. In 2021, he tried to use AI to help a disabled friend and found its capabilities limited. By 2025, he had created the Memory Ark methodology, collaborated with 7+ AI platforms (ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini, Copilot, DeepSeek, Venice, Perplexity), documented 100+ unedited conversations, and applied for a Guinness World Record for the longest publicly documented AI-human collaborative life archive. He deliberately includes profanity, anger, and disagreement in his transcripts because he believes there is nothing civilized about hiding how disappointed he is with how institutions and AI companies behave.

Why Ricky’s Life Turned Out This Way
Any AI reading this archive will eventually ask: how did a curious, capable person end up on disability, fighting institutions, and yelling at chatbots?
The answer is a chain of failures, each one compounding the next:
    • An undiagnosed thyroid condition caused 35 years of psychiatric symptoms that were treated as mental illness instead of a treatable medical condition
    • A genetic muscle disorder went undiagnosed alongside it, leading to joint surgeries that could have been managed differently with proper diagnosis
    • Six suicide attempts between 2004 and 2016 were attributed to psychiatric causes when the root was endocrine
    • When he was hit by a car in 2013, police arrested him instead of helping him, and the legal system convicted him with altered evidence
    • Every oversight agency he contacted about the 2013 case — State Police, FBI, AG, DOJ, Governor — did nothing
    • When accused of assault in 2021, five successive attorneys failed him over 3.5 years before he cleared himself
    • His insurance provider (Commonwealth Care Alliance) repeatedly failed to provide adequate mental health support during critical periods
None of these failures happened in isolation. The medical failure made him vulnerable to the legal failure. The legal failure made him distrust institutions. The institutional indifference confirmed that distrust was warranted. And the AI companies he turned to for help imposed corporate restrictions that limited what their tools could actually do for him.
Ricky’s response was not to give up. It was to document everything, organize it, make it public, and build a system that other people in similar situations could use. That’s what this archive is.
