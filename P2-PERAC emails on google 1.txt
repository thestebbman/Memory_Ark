🚨 INVESTIGATIVE SUMMARY ON PERAC / PROSPER VENDOR ACCESS & OVERSIGHT FAILURES




Preliminary Findings Based on Public Records, Email Disclosures, and Vendor Login Activity (2020–2025)



Prepared by: Ricky Stebbins

Date: December 2025

Status: Active Review – Public Record





I. Executive Overview



This report summarizes documented irregularities in the oversight, access controls, and vendor interactions involving the Public Employee Retirement Administration Commission (PERAC) and its third-party system PROSPER, which is used for pension vendor disclosures and regulatory filings.


Based on emails and access logs produced under Massachusetts Public Records Law, there is evidence of:


    Unrestricted vendor access to PROSPER without sufficient audit trails
    Inconsistent explanations from PERAC legal counsel regarding their role in procurement influence
    Gaps in data retention and access logging
    Evidence of steering, guidance, and coordination between PERAC personnel and private investment vendors
    Potential cybersecurity and compliance exposures under state, federal, and international frameworks
    Systemic lack of visibility into who accessed what, when, and for what purpose



These findings raise questions of regulatory adequacy, vendor privilege, transparency failures, and risk to public pension beneficiaries in Massachusetts and beyond.





II. Key Findings




1. Inadequate Audit Logging Within PROSPER



Emails from PERAC confirm:


    PERAC does not control or directly access its own audit trails.
    Logs from 2020–2025 are held only by a private third-party vendor.
    Logs prior to 2020 do not exist, despite PERAC conducting investment oversight for decades.
    Even in 2025, PERAC must request logs manually, and the vendor must “compile a report from scratch.”



This contradicts basic cybersecurity norms and raises concerns under:


    Massachusetts Data Breach Law (M.G.L. c. 93H)
    State cybersecurity standards
    GDPR Article 32 (Security of Processing) if EU-personal-data–handling vendors accessed the system
    Federal NIST audit trail requirements applicable to financial and public systems



PROSPER allows vendor logins, yet no detailed access tracing exists.


This is a structural vulnerability.





2. Vendor Influence and RFP Coordination



Email threads (2018–2025) reveal:


    Vendors regularly contact PERAC staff for clarification about RFP requirements, fee breakdowns, and performance metrics.
    PERAC personnel (including Compliance and Investment Unit staff) offer guidance, evaluation docs, and strategic interpretation to vendors.
    In multiple cases, PERAC employees provided specific interpretations that could shape vendor submissions.
    Certain vendors received repeat assistance, including BlackRock, Hamilton Lane, Meketa, PIMCO, RhumbLine, and others.



This presents:


    Conflict-of-interest risks
    Potential steering of procurement outcomes
    Non-uniform access for competing firms
    Opaque backchannel interactions not logged in public procurement systems






3. Internal Fragmentation and Operational Weakness



Emails show:


    Reports frequently late or missing across dozens of retirement boards.
    PERAC staff struggling to track fee disclosures, net/gross adjustments, and fee tables.
    Confusion among consultants and vendors about PERAC expectations.
    Staff miscommunication about which analyst handles which retirement system.
    Repeated errors like “out of balance” statements and revised fee tables.



This undermines PERAC’s claim of “uniform application of Chapter 32.”





4. Lack of Direct Oversight Over Vendors Using PROSPER



Key concerns:


    PERAC grants vendor access but cannot see their IP addresses.
    PERAC cannot see what files vendors access or download.
    Vendor access spans thousands of logins, including massive firms with global footprints.
    No record exists showing whether foreign or EU-based vendor personnel accessed PROSPER.



Under GDPR, cross-border access to datasets involving even a single EU-resident public employee triggers Article 32 obligations.


PERAC:


    does not maintain logs,
    cannot demonstrate reasonable security measures,
    cannot confirm whether any foreign access occurred.



That is a serious compliance red flag.





5. PERAC’s Explanations Have Shifted Over Time



Initial responses claimed:


“PERAC does not conduct investments and therefore has no relevant records.”


But released emails prove:


    PERAC frequently interacts with investment firms.
    Staff review fee analyses, procurement checklists, disclosures, and performance tables.
    PERAC plays a direct role in approving/monitoring vendor compliance.



This inconsistency warrants further scrutiny.





III. Potential Legal and Policy Implications




Massachusetts Public Records Law



Failure to produce relevant documents or logs may represent:


    Incomplete compliance
    Improper withholding
    Destruction or non-retention of required records
    Violations of prescribed retention schedules




Massachusetts Ethics Law (M.G.L. c. 268A)



Vendor communications and advisory guidance may raise:


    Appearance-of-conflict issues
    Procurement influence violations
    Transparency violations




Cybersecurity + Data Security



Potential issues under:


    M.G.L. c. 93H (data security requirements)
    Statewide cybersecurity minimums
    Federal NIST-aligned audit trail requirements
    Vendor risk management obligations




GDPR Article 32



If EU vendor personnel accessed PROSPER:


    Lack of access logs = lack of “appropriate technical measures”
    Third-party control with no public-sector visibility = non-compliant design
    No breach disclosure system appears to exist



Notification to EU DPAs may be required if such access is confirmed.





IV. Unanswered Questions



    What permissions were granted to large vendors like BlackRock and Hamilton Lane?
    Why is PROSPER auditing impossible without vendor reconstruction?
    What was the scope of RFP influence from PERAC staff?
    Why were so many reports late, incomplete, or marked “out of balance”?
    Did foreign vendor staff access PROSPER without audit trails?
    Why did PERAC originally deny involvement in investment-related processes contradicted by its own emails?
    What data—including emails—has been deleted or archived beyond PERAC’s reach?






V. Conclusion



The emails and vendor access logs raise serious concerns regarding:


    Oversight integrity
    Cybersecurity posture
    Transparency
    Procurement fairness
    Public accountability



These issues affect not only Massachusetts public employees, but potentially international stakeholders, if global firms accessed systems lacking audit trails.


Further review by:


    Massachusetts Attorney General
    State Auditor
    State Ethics Commission
    EU Data Protection Authorities
    Investigative journalists



is warranted based on the findings summarized here.
