March




I remember being such a dork during this school year; Ghostbusters Two was coming out. I remember talking to the same kid, Matt O, from the talent show and another classmate, Melissa. I wanna say her last name's Moriarty. lol I'm pretty sure her mom was a teacher there across the hall from Mrs. Benoit.

Anyway, the 3 us used to talk about making Ghostbusters costumes, exploring the school, and looking for ghosts. lol I was such a dork. Now that I'm an adult, I love dressing up for comic-cons. lol Back then it was easy to find slime, and we would bring it to school and pretend that ghosts left it. We'd leave it in the windows over by the stairs that led up to the kitchen. Then we'd tell other kids we found something.

July



When I was younger, I used to wet the bed. This thought randomly popped into my head while walking with Harley by my old school, O.L.S.H.; this was August 6, 2026. 
I used to be a very deep sleeper and have super vivid dreams, like I was there. I could replay scenes in my mind; even though they're vague in my mind's eye as an adult, it felt real. I've started crying in dreams and woken up to find I still had tears running down my face; it was sore. 





 The Cape Cod stuff was around 1988-1992?



I'm not sure why this memory popped into my head, but we used to go camping a lot as kids. When my uncle Chet moved out to Cape Cod while he was a recruiter for the Army, we went to visit every summer and stayed there for days. 

I thought it was the greatest when my dad, brother, and I got to sleep in our tent in their backyard, but I remember wetting myself and needing to hang my sleeping bag out on a clothesline. 

I recall Chet being his typical self and trying to make me feel stupid and like a baby for peeing in my sleep. 

I always felt like he was a jerk for no reason; he just liked to act like he was better than everyone else, but couldn't explain himself or give me a path to improve myself. You were just a piece of trash in his eyes and not worth the effort, or at least that's how I recall feeling. lol

The last few times I had accidents, I didn't get the bed wet, but I remember dreaming of getting up to use the bathroom, and when I felt the sensation, I immediately awoke and rushed to the bathroom, which still required a change of clothes or just underwear, but it wasn't something I could fight. Once I stopped having those super vivid dreams, I stopped having accidents. I wonder if it was all the fluoride. lol They used to have people come in to give us little cups of it to swish around and spit out. I remember thinking it tasted great. I always looked forward to them coming in since it was a distraction from our normal routine.

They also used to have people come in to check our spines to make sure they were growing straight and checked our hair for lice. 







I also remember getting berated; instead of having a conversation, I peed with the seat down, left some drops behind, I guess, and left without wiping the seat. lol I totally remember standing there peeing, but I don't recall the seat being something on my mind. This is another time that adults just bitched and moaned, so the lesson went right over my head and I learned nothing from certain people, except maybe to wonder more about what the hell people were thinking when they said things. 

This only sticks with me because my uncle Chet wanted to let me know what a shitty person I was and what a disappointment I was. lol So why not share it with others?

One time we got to see Chet and my cousin Ray, his adopted son, do a road race. I can't recall what place they came in or if Ray beat Chet, but it got the idea of running with friends into my head, though it would be decades before I was healthy enough to do it. 

There was a cranberry bog at the end of his street, and I loved to explore and walk the paths, but we never brought toys or played in those areas. lol I probably would've gotten in trouble and had a fleet of toy boats defending the area. 



This is also one of the few times I recall going ocean fishing with my dad and brother. I don't recall anyone else going. My dad bought these worms with pincers for teeth (sandworms). I think we caught small flounder on a few of these trips. I don't recall going on many ocean fishing trips as a child, but these worms were very cool and unlike anything I got to see around Springfield. 

We also went camping at some site across from a different cranberry bog. I can't recall much about this place. I think we slept in tents, but we might have used a camper. I just remember there wasn't much to do, so I did a lot of exploring, but there was nothing exciting to mess with. 

====

They moved to Kansas after the Cape, before finally moving to Texas. I think Grandma Francis went to visit them there, but I'll have to find out. I remember talking on the phone during holidays and people mentioning the long-distance charges. I wasn't sure what that meant or why people seemed to care; I just loved talking to people on the phone.

Aunt Flossie had a rotary-dial phone for decades. I loved spinning that before making a call. I can picture my little fingers slipping and screwing up, so I'd have to start over. It was always my turn as a kid. I wanted to go first. I don't remember volunteering to let my brother have a turn until somewhere around this time. The concept of letting someone else enjoy what I was enjoying didn't click for me. 





====

They had the cutest small dog: it was bigger than Harley, but I remember it died after they moved to Texas. I remember my uncle Chet telling me something like it was too hot down there for it, whatever that meant. Looking back, I think he's always acted like an insensitive person. Seeing as he left his other dog, Daisy, out in the yard all the time. I don't think he loved those dogs or treated them like part of the family. He is too cool to play with man's best friend. lol



I know I poscrewed-upl the screwed up stuff people do to each other and how I perceive it, but there are two sides to every story, and I only know how to tell things from one perspective. no Itter how hard to try to see things from other people's perspectives, I can't, I don't really have a clue where they're coming from and I can only use my own experience to try to decode them. Sometimes I guess right or am close. Other times I'm way off and totally misread the person. 

==========



I think this was between 6th and 7th grade; either way, lol. It was that time of year, my brother and I were tasked with cleaning our room, and my dad found the sandwiches my brother had been hiding under his dresser. lol 



Our mom used to make us a peanut butter and jelly or a baloney and cheese sandwich for lunch every day, and it got boring. I'm not sure why my brother thought saving them was a great idea, or maybe he was afraid to toss them in the trash; either way, he decided to keep them close, and when my dad moved his bureau out of the way, there were like 15-20 moldy sandwiches stuffed underneath it. lol They were all nice and fuzzy; a few looked like slime. lol

I remember laughing my as off when I heard my dad ttellBobby he was going to have to eat those sandwiches and started crying. lol I'm laughing as I type this. 

 Obviously, my dad wasn't serious, but this is part of where my dark sense of humor came from. I love playing harmless jokes on people and freaking them out.

Summer


This is the summer that I brought my GI Joe Night Force Hovercraft to the family picnic at the Hampden Rec and floated it down the stream. 

This was the coolest time I've ever had playing with my GI Joes away from home.

 The stream there isn't huge or fast-moving, so it's perfect for kids playing with toys.

They also have a tunnel that runs underneath the road that leads to the water park, so naturally we would crawl through that every year, and the hovercraft was ready for the job.

Allowing the hovercraft to float through it on its own was like allowing it to go on its own mission. With all my cousins there, I was able to have them follow it, and I ran over the top and excitedly waited for it to pop out the other end and crash into the pool we created at the end.

 This requires a little explaining, so I added an old Google Earth pic below. 





The stream exits somewhere off to the right of the road that leads into the park. The stream follows the trail of trees parallel to the road leading to the right-hand turn. The stream runs under the leads to the waterpark. The stream runs north between the parking lot on the left and the picnic/play area with sand or mulch on the right. The stream veers to the right; it separates the two softball fields.

We would always dam up the stream using large rocks; it would really slow the flow of the water and create a pool we could play in. The dam was about 15 feet after the tunnel. 

These events were so much fun, 
I wanted to do something similar as an adult with the hovercraft I bought in 2020/21, but I didn't get a chance to float it down any streams.


===

Every summer when we go to New York, we would stop by my aunt Connie's. She had a hot dog stand, I guess you'd call it, on the side of the road. It was called Connie's Michigan's. People would come over from all over to eat there, even from Canada.  It was a well-known place at the time. I loved going there.

After she passed, the family stopped running the business. I can't remember if they closed right away, but we would pass that location visiting other family members every year, so it was always something I would ask about.






So here’s a funny, messed up story. I was like 11 or 12 years old, and we were up at my Aunt Pearl's house in New York, not her camp. I was playing near the garage with my Go-Bots, and this older female cousin (second cousin-twice removed lol) grabbed me and kissed me. 

One of the adults saw it through the window and threw a fit; it was all her doing, yet I got blamed for that nonsense, not her. 

All I cared about was playing with my toys in the cedar trees and lilac bushes that grew in her yard.



Those dirty bastards blamed me. Adults are so dumb sometimes.



I never forgot that.

It’s probably one of the reasons I never make the first move now. I just wait. I think that moment wired something in me.



That stuff sticks with you, even if it doesn’t seem big at the time.

Christmas this year I remember buying the Batman game for the Commodore 64. I tried my hardest to get past a certain level, but I kept getting stuck in a stairwell, so I was never able to drive the Batmobile or fly the Batwing. I got so frustrated with that game. It sucked that there wasn't any game guide. I remember trying on and off for years and experiencing nothing but disappointment. I was heartbroken and wanted to try those vehicle levels so badly. Which is funny because now it doesn't interest me at all. I'd rather play games with other people, like Mario Kart or StarFox. 

This is when I really remember starting sledding at Lynch, 

===
I really struggled to find my place in school. I didn't feel like I fit in, and I felt like people always talked to me like I was a disappointment, lol 
Mr. Kohrhamer was one of the harder teachers to deal with emotionally as a kid. Our personalities clashed, so I'd be curious to hear how it was or experience dealing with that version of me. It was one thing to constantly listen to women who can't relate to you, but it's different when men can't either. 

One time I recall standing in the hallway; there were a couple of teachers standing there near Mr. Kohrhamer while he was trying to reprimand me, and he told me I was like dealing with an alcoholic. lol That clearly stuck with me. 


I would love to have a deep conversation with all of my old teachers now to debate all of this, just to see who has better reasoning skills. lol Did they teach me to think this way, or did I learn to think this way because of how I was taught?

I think some of my teachers' lack of patience and compassion over my life really inspired me to learn more about humans and why they don't think about their actions, even though they can think about them before making them. lol.

I should probably elaborate on things. I might not be the brightest, but I currently try to acknowledge what's going on around me and what I'm forced to ignore, so I think this is less about how I feel I was treated and more about how I think we can do something to make sure all kids get treated better in the future.

I don't have specific stories about all the times I was told to stop talking or enough with the questions; we're moving on.


I remember feeling like I was always annoying my teachers, no matter what I did. I don't know if I was, or if I was just feeling that way. 
  



 




December 

When The Simpsons debuted, I was grounded for something I did at school. lol 
I remember trying to "sneak watch" it in from the hallway, like everyone couldn't hear me creep out of my room. I remember feeling like I was missing out, even though it's just a dumb cartoon, it felt like it was an event, we . We eventually all watched it as a family. We had a VCR and recorded episodes all the time.

I'm not sure if this show is what started my love of prank calling people or if it was another, but there are episodes like the Al-Coholic and the Amanda-Hug-and-Kiss that still make me laugh, and I'm sure I'd prank call people today if I couldn't get caught. lol

I may have spoken about *67 and pranking other people once my uncle Mike and cousins moved to Moss Rd. We had access to phones upstairs, so unless they picked up while we were harassing someone, our chances of getting caught were slim. So we would prank people all the time and have full conversations pretending to be other people. Even now it sounds hilarious to call someone and pretend to be drunk, then get them all worked up over nothing. Some poor guy would get a call and be totally caught off guard by the nonsense that I had caused.
