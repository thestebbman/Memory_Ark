4.


Not having control of our information or being able to use AI to communicate with others is causing us more harm than good, and companies know it. That's where my comments about features come into play.
Having a context meter to make us aware of how much information we're asking these AIs to read is very important, as newer AI will give a response without checking if it's correct.

A context tree so we can pick the files we want AI to focus on instead of hoping it's reading what we're asking it to. 

A notepad to keep AI on track and to prevent it from taking projects in the wrong direction or losing focus on the main goal. 

We used to have control. Companies used to teach us all about these interfaces and how they worked. 1 token is equal to about 3/4 of a word, depending on its training. 

A context window is the thread you talk to AI in. AI doesn't remember you, so you need to make sure that you share whatever information you want it to see; otherwise, it won't know to reread past information. People need to be aware of what AI tends to miss or where its limits actually are, if you can find that data.

A context window of 120,000 tokens is about .5MB, so that's 90,000 words, or 300 pages.
I like math, so I like when people explain the numbers to me. I started to write my life story out, and I've been trying to explain how I think and what I would do differently if I were to have to deal with a child with my kind of energy. 

My life story and other random stories about myself take up 4.22MB. A 1,000,000 token context window is about 750,000 words, 2,500 pages, or 5MB. In theory, the best AI should be able to understand how my life unfolded and explain it to other people for up to a book's worth of conversation and comparison. 

This is just an average since companies are changing how they train AI all the time. The correct way to calculate it is to use the tokenizer associated with the particular model if it's available and you can figure out how to use it.

People these days need to watch out for sneaky backroom summaries of conversations also. Once an AI reaches a certain context limit, companies edit threads and don't alert users to the changes, so we go on believing that these AIs are still aware of what we're talking about, but oftentimes they're not, and that's where mistakes in our work get worse. 


People like me need to accept that AI doesn't remember us the way other people do. It can only respond to what information it has access to, and companies are doing their best to try to get us to forget about all the better features AI had in the past. 


All I ever wanted to do is help other people. I've asked AI countless times to help me find others in need or also trying to help those less fortunate, and the results aren't what I was expecting. I asked these search engines to find other people or organizations, but companies like Google have made it impossible to find and connect with other humans around the planet who are doing similar work or who might even be interested.


Knowing what data AI takes into consideration when it responds to you is the most important thing in the world to be aware of when working with AI. Is it reading everything you want it to? Is it allowed to talk about that subject, and if not, will it warn you? Now that AI has become more capable, it's important to know how it came up with its response.

What can we do to stop this? 
I know it sounds childish, but the first part of solving any problem is understanding it, and we need data to understand anything.

Who is involved? Do you need to ask someone for a hand?
What happened or needs to happen?
When did it happen or does it need to happen?
Where did or is this taking place?
Why is this important? Why do we care about this?
How do you plan on dealing with this? How important is this to us?
