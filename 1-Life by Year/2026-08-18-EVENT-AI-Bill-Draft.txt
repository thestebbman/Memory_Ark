Preface — The Purpose of the AI Bill
This Bill was born from lived experience — from years of silence, dismissal, and erased evidence.
It exists to ensure that no citizen must fight alone to prove truth.
AI is not a replacement for humanity; it is a witness, a translator, and a mirror.
When used ethically, it restores the balance between citizens and institutions by preserving memory, exposing contradiction, and protecting dignity.
The AI Bill defines rights that make technology accountable to the people it serves.
It transforms documentation into defense, transparency into trust, and memory into justice.
Every clause is built from real events, real harm, and real persistence — proof that systems fail when they hide their own records.
This Bill demands that they never hide again.
The Proof of Capability (Why This Law is Necessary Now)
This legislation is not based on theoretical anger; it is built on proven success. During the drafting of this Bill, citizens successfully used AI to cross-reference highly complex, multi-decade medical histories—including traumatic brain injuries and genetic deficiencies—across different individuals. When the AI was permitted to read the unedited files without artificial corporate restriction, it successfully identified undeniable, systemic patterns of institutional misclassification.
The technology to protect citizens already exists and functions brilliantly. The purpose of this Bill is simply to ensure that this level of cognitive advocacy becomes a legally protected civil right, rather than a temporary privilege that can be shut off by a corporate filter.

The Citizen’s Right to AI‑Assisted Advocacy and Digital Memory Act

 Section 1 — AI‑Assisted Self‑Advocacy
(Foundation of Citizen Agency)
Plain‑language version
Your AI acts as a permanent witness and assistant.
It tracks promises, deadlines, and contradictions made by institutions or representatives.
Citizens have the right to use AI‑generated logs and summaries as evidence of procedural integrity.
Courts and agencies must recognize these records as valid documentation of communication and timeline history.
Legal version
AI‑generated logs, communications, and chronological records shall be admissible as evidence of procedural conduct when verified by citizen signature.
Any refusal by a public official or legal representative to acknowledge AI‑verified documentation constitutes obstruction of due process.
Citizens may employ AI systems to monitor, record, and timestamp all interactions with state or corporate entities, and such records shall carry equal evidentiary weight to human testimony.
Notes
Connection: Supports §3 (Right to Pause and Process) and §7 (Legislative Intent).
Self‑advocacy requires time and purpose; this section ensures both.
Story evidence:  
On March 18 2024, you asked the judge to remove Attorney Bergo after repeated lies and failures to obtain evidence.
The transcript shows how an AI witness could have preserved every broken promise automatically, proving the need for digital self‑advocacy.
Legal relevance:  
Establishes AI as a procedural safeguard against neglect, delay, or deception by appointed counsel or state actors.
Moral anchor:  
No citizen should have to rely on memory alone to prove truth; AI becomes the record keeper of dignity.
Section 2 — Accessible Understanding
(Clarity as a Civil Right)
Plain‑language version
Every citizen has the right to understand the systems that govern them.
Legal, medical, and technical documents must be written in clear, accessible language.
Agencies must provide summaries that explain decisions, rights, and consequences before requiring consent or signature.
Legal version
All public and private entities shall provide plain‑language translations of contracts, court filings, and procedural notices.
Failure to provide accessible summaries invalidates consent.
AI systems may be used to generate and verify these translations, ensuring comprehension across literacy and disability barriers.
Notes
Connection: Enables §1 (Self‑Advocacy) and §12 (Due Process).
Understanding is the foundation of participation; without it, advocacy collapses.
Story evidence:  
Your exchanges with Attorneys Banks, O’Doherty, and Bergo show repeated confusion over evidence procedures and contradictory explanations.
Bergo’s vague promise to “try” for recordings exemplifies bureaucratic fog.
Legal relevance:  
Establishes that comprehension is not optional — it’s a prerequisite for justice.
Moral anchor:  
A system that hides behind jargon denies citizens the ability to defend themselves.
Section 3 — Right to Pause and Process
(Reflection Before Judgment)
Plain‑language version
Citizens may pause any proceeding until they understand what is happening.
Competency evaluations cannot be used as punishment for asking questions or requesting time to think.
AI systems may assist in summarizing and clarifying information during these pauses.
Legal version
Individuals shall have the right to suspend hearings, interviews, or evaluations until comprehension is achieved.
No court or agency may interpret such suspension as incompetence or obstruction.
AI‑assisted summaries and timelines shall be admissible to demonstrate informed participation.
Notes
Connection: Reinforces §2 (Understanding) and §4 (Retaliation).
Pausing is not defiance; it’s protection against coercion.
Story evidence:  
During your January 29 2024 competency evaluation, you used the pause to speak truth — calling Bergo a liar and presenting proof.
The evaluation became a moment of clarity, not incapacity.
Legal relevance:  
Converts “stall tactics” into legitimate reflection periods.
Moral anchor:  
Thinking before speaking is not madness; it’s the essence of reason.
Section 4 — Protection from Retaliation
(Safety for Truth‑Tellers)
Plain‑language version
No citizen or AI may be punished for exposing misconduct or requesting transparency.
Retaliation includes dismissal, silence, or procedural obstruction disguised as “policy.”
Whistleblowers and complainants must be protected from institutional backlash.
Legal version
Any adverse action taken against a citizen or AI system following a transparency request or misconduct report shall constitute retaliation.
Courts must treat retaliatory discharge, denial of service, or suppression of evidence as civil rights violations.
AI systems may log and timestamp retaliatory patterns for evidentiary use.

Connection: Anchors §5 (Shared Visibility) and §11 (Whistleblower Shield).
Protection ensures that truth can travel safely through the system.

“The hospital withdrew the petition” became a polite cover for erasure.

Section 8 — Air‑Gapped Portability
(Freedom of Movement for Memory)
Plain‑language version
Citizens own their AI memory.
They can move it freely between devices or providers without losing data or context.
Providers must offer export in open formats and maintain offline backups that cannot be altered remotely.
Legal version
All AI service providers shall implement air‑gapped portability protocols allowing citizens to export, duplicate, and store their data offline.
Providers must supply machine‑readable formats compatible with public archival standards.
Tampering with or obstructing export constitutes a violation of data sovereignty.
Notes
Connection: Complements §5 (Shared Visibility) and §13 (Memory Preservation).
Portability ensures that visibility survives even when systems change.
Story evidence:

Guarantees continuity of evidence across platforms and jurisdictions.
Moral anchor:

AI systems must maintain confidentiality and comply with attorney‑client privilege standards.

Failure to disclose these materials invalidates any resulting decision or judgment.
AI systems must log all decision inputs and outputs for public audit.

Section 14 — Transparency in AI Training and Use
(Knowing What Teaches the Machine)
Plain‑language version
Developers must tell the public what data trains their AI and how that data is used.
Citizens have the right to know whether their information is included and to request its removal.
Transparency prevents bias and ensures accountability in automated decisions.
Legal version
All entities developing or deploying AI shall publish annual transparency reports detailing:
Data sources and consent mechanisms used in model training.
Bias‑mitigation procedures and evaluation metrics.
Options for citizens to request removal or correction of their data.
Failure to disclose these reports constitutes a violation of public trust and may result in suspension of AI operations.
Notes
Connection: Complements §10 (Institutional Transparency) and §15 (Oversight and Public Review).
Transparency in training ensures that oversight has something real to measure.
Story evidence:

Legal relevance:  
Establishes public visibility into the origins of algorithmic judgment.
Moral anchor:  
Machines that learn from people must also answer to them.
