This summer we went to Fern Lake, then to Buffalo, Niagara Falls. I got to meet my great-grandparents, Harold and Helen Johnson, and my grandfather, James Landers. His biological father died long before I was born, so I never met him, and his mother remarried. 



We used to watch WWF wrestling and Gorgeous Ladies of Wrestling all the time. I loved the wild female characters. Everything was so over the top back then, and my mind soaked it up like a sponge. 






2nd grade 1985-1986 

It was around this time that my dad bought a Commodore 64 computer. We used to play that thing all the time, and we stunk at it. There were no games that were worth playing. It didn't really teach us anything. Though I did learn how to make lists and summaries of my comics and stored them on a floppy disk. 
 






And the pictures above are of my grandpa Landers, my brother, sister, and me, and in a lower picture, my great-grandparents, the Johnsons. That was Grandpa Landers’s mother and his stepfather.

All I have are positive memories of the visits with my mother’s father and grandparents. Though from what I’ve been able to get out of my mother, her grandmother, my great-grandmother, was not a very pleasant woman, and neither was her father/my grandfather.
I remember playing in the parking lot next door to their house in Batavia, New York, and going to Niagara Falls. I always remember being super excited to go visit people. But if you look at the pictures, my grandfather looks like a crabby ass, whiny baby in all of them. 

(I remember calling him after my daughter was born in 1997, and being all excited. I really wanted to visit him, but he wasn't interested in a family visit. I’m pretty sure that was the last time I talked to him as well.)


This is the same year(I think, lol) that we got that PUCH Moped that was made in October 1978. I thought that was the greatest thing as a child, and we kept that until I think 2019 or 2020.  When my dad finally nagged me about it enough that I agreed to give it away while we were living in Warren.

November


It was in computer class that I first got to play with a form of AI. Sister Margaret is who taught us about context windows, files, folders, indexes, summaries, threads, and running out of tokens. They had a room full of Apple II computers, and they were networked together. 
We got to have a conversation with these early chatbots, and if we wasted the time and space we had, then the conversation didn't result in anything useful. I thought it was more fun playing Oregon Trail and dying from dysentery than it was getting nowhere, having the same conversation over and over again with these early forms of AI. Keith was always better at all these games and talking to these programs than I was. He was the computer guy in our class. There was always a point to these chatbot conversations, and I could never get them to say what I needed to in order to succeed. 
If anyone was going to work with AI from grade school, I think it would have been Keith. Though he passed away in 2004. I went over to his house once to play and got to see his Transformers, and I remember being so excited that he had Wheeljack. Keith's personality reminds me of him too. 


It was so much fun learning how to fail at speaking to these chatbots. Everything was about building something to explain what we're talking about. We used paper and clear plastic squares to show context in a literal window. Sister Margaret used the term context window to explain what literal context inside of a space meant and how easy it is to take something out of context when it isn't inside the window. 

We had two examples. There was a sheet of craft paper and the window attached, then a sheet of paper was pulled through like a scroll. What we call a thread, she called a scroll; once the information you were talking about scrolled past the window, you weren't able to use it again. The tokens weren't words; they were how many times you had to get the questions or conversation right. They were like quarters to a game. The context window was how many words fit in a space at a time, so I knew what the computer did and didn't see.

The second context window was more of a game. We had the clear plastic square, but we could slide it around and pick which words and phrases we wanted to use; we had to use the words and phrases inside the window, and we were limited to how long our papers could be. 

They weren't teaching us to work with AI. They were teaching us to say what we mean and mean what we say, which we don't often do.

December


I also remember watching The He-Man and the Masters of the Universe Christmas special that year with my cousins. I think we watched this at my grandfather's. There were so many of us watching it and running wild that I can't recall where; I only remember watching it and being super excited and wishing I had powers. 

This Christmas, I got the MASK Thunder Hawk from my cousin Ellie and Billy. I remember she tried to buy it at Toys R Us, and I wasn't supposed to see it. But I'm nosy as hell and notice everything, so instead of ignoring what I saw, I asked her, "Hey, what's this?" and she tried to pretend to return it. I also got the GI Joe Mauler for Christmas. We collected garbage pail kids, and I got in trouble for bringing those to school.
