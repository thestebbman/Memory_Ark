Uncle Art 

I remember Uncle Art dying on March 23,1983, and nobody ever talking about it again; it's like he just vanished. It was the weirdest thing in the world. Which is also why I don't have many stories about him. 

This was the first death in my family that I actually recall as an event. I have a vague memory of going over to the casket, but the entire situation was confusing to me. 

Years later I learned that he had a heart attack while out helping deliver newspapers for his daughter. Doris. She worked that job for 40-ish years of my life. I'm not sure if she was a manager and dealt with bulk deliveries that sent their shipments to area delivery people, but eventually I got a paper route, and she dropped off my stack. 

Years later.
I had heard that my aunt Flossie blamed Doris for Art's heart attack, and now, looking back, I'm curious if I misunderstood what someone said, and maybe she felt like Art was overexerting himself, since slinging stacks of newspapers is a bit of exercise. 



I can't recall how long after his funeral. We were going to church, and I remember wanting to see his spirit. I felt like I wanted to know that he was in a better place and was expecting something. I remember walking into the middle bedroom and seeing a shadow and wishing it was him. I was hoping it would react to me; I was hoping to find a connection with it. lol But it was just a shadow. It wasn't like those figures in Colonial Estates.

I think this is the first time that I really noticed that no one talked about people after they passed away, and it was more like a burden. Everyone was sad and depressed, and no one ever celebrated anyone's life. No one passed on any positive morals or values, or any stories that would inspire me to better myself.



I also noticed that some of the light went out of my Aunt Flossie after Uncle Art died. She was always a light in my life, but you could tell she lost a part of her. I never got to hear her tell stories about Art. She did talk about other things, but Art she held close to her heart. 

I know my uncle Chet would always complain and have something negative to say about Uncle Art's drinking, but they were always vague stories.

Another random story. 30-ish years later. Maybe after Aunt Flossie's funeral?  My dad told me that I had spit at my Uncle Art, or in his face, and Art slapped me across the face. My dad mentioned my Aunt Flossie having words with Art and putting him in his place. Which makes me wonder why Art felt like I needed a slap. lol I am so curious what caused me to do that, but I'll never know. 


I have no idea why this stuck with me, but I've always been curious about behavior, and I know I copied a lot of things I've seen on TV. It might sound mean, but a real slap in the face will teach you what a TV slap in the face should feel like. lol, and it will hopefully teach people not to do it to each other out of anger.

TV

Man, this is where it all started. I used to be obsessed with TV.

I loved watching shows like Winnie the Pooh and Fraggle Rock; it was also sometime around here that my love of music and theme songs in particular really developed. I was watching a lot of TV, but I also remember my dad playing the guitar; his brother, Uncle Dave, and my dad's best friend Rick Moran also played the guitar. 

I also remember watching Jaws around this time. I was probably too young, lol, but I would've snuck peeks, even if the adults said no. Jaws is why I'm afraid of the water to this day. lol, even in a pond, my heart starts pounding, and I imagine a shark could get me. lol 

I don't feel dumb about any of these ideas; people make Sharknado movies, so I know other adults think crazy stuff.

I also remember watching The Exorcist at Aunt Flossie's on Ray ST. lol seeing that girl's head twist around, then projectile vomit gave me nightmares for the longest time. I remember being fascinated and terrified at the idea of someone being possessed by evil spirits. Though I always imagined the good guys winning, I didn't imagine being consumed, lol

I remember my dad's friend Rick buying my brother Rambo toys. I thought it was the coolest thing in the world; they had rocket launchers and machine guns. My brother and I played with these things like they were real, and needless to say, I was really rough with them, so they didn't last.

I used to have a blast throwing He-Man, Skeletor, Fisto, and anybody else that would get caught up in the clump of vines that was growing up the ground wire to the antenna, on the side of the house, in our driveway. The figures would get for days, and I'd have to wait until a windstorm for them to fall down; it was so exciting to walk outside and see that somebody was finally free from the Vine Trap. lol

Summer



In the picture below, the top left corner is my brother and I playing next to the hatchway on Hope Street; the top right corner is me on a walkway at Fern Lake in New York.

The bottom left corner is my brother and I dressed up in Halloween costumes, and the other three are my brother and I in the General Lee. That's Ellie with us in the car. I think this is the right year. Either way, I absolutely loved the General Lee.

 Just like everybody else, I thought it was the coolest thing in the world, and someone over by Duggan Middle School owned one. I'm not sure if Ellie and Billy were friends with the owners or if we just stopped by and they were friendly, but I really do feel like we got lucky to experience all these different events in the 1980's. 


My cousin Ellie and Billy lived over on Bretton Rd, in Springfield, for years; that's how they met Mark Festa. He was our tax guy up until 2019. 










My brother and I got to sleep over at Ellie and Billy's house a few times when they lived on Bretton Rd. I remember racing General Lee Matchbox and other Hot Wheels/Matchbox cars down a wooden ramp in their basement. I recall having so much fun that I made an improved version of that ramp for Dominic and me to race our Hot Wheels down in 2012. Ours has rails on its sides to prevent falls. This seemed so much more exciting once they came out with all the movie- and TV-themed cars and monster trucks.

September



This fall I started going to Mary M. Lynch for kindergarten. I don't remember the start of this year being very stimulating. Nothing sticks out. 



 
I took these pictures of the front and side of the school in 2026.
I don't remember much from my time here. 

I'm curious whether knowing my grandparents lived at the top of the hill outside, plus having the playground where I got to goof around with cousins right outside the classroom window, distracted me from paying attention in class, or if it wasn't an issue for me.
