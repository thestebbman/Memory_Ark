# Journalist Outreach Email
## Re: Graham v. DA Hampden (SJC-13386) — Ground-Level Documentation

---

**SUBJECT:** SJC-13386: Hampden DA Brady violations — a victim with 12 years of documentation and a live case

---

My name is Richard Stebbins. I am a Springfield, Massachusetts resident who has been documenting misconduct by the Hampden County District Attorney's office since 2013. I am writing because the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court's January 2024 ruling in *Graham v. District Attorney for the Hampden District* (SJC-13386) validated — in the Commonwealth's own highest court — exactly what I have been reporting to every oversight agency in Massachusetts for over a decade.

**What the SJC found:**

The court found that the Hampden County DA's office committed three separate Brady violations in its handling of Springfield Police Department misconduct evidence: selectively disclosing adverse credibility findings about officers, withholding misconduct evidence when it couldn't be attributed to a single officer, and failing to obtain and disclose DOJ investigation materials. The DOJ had previously found that Springfield PD officers — particularly in the narcotics bureau — "routinely falsified police reports" and engaged in a "pattern or practice of excessive force." The DA's office's response to the SJC: "Everybody knows what everybody knows. There are no secrets in Springfield."

**What that looked like on the ground:**

On January 16, 2013, I was struck by a vehicle in a parking lot. When police arrived, I was arrested without being allowed to give a statement and denied medical attention. I wrote a notarized statement afterward and submitted it to the DA's office. That statement was later altered — two pages deleted, my signature electronically forged — and entered into evidence. The receipt the DA's office submitted as proof of payment was a forgery; I obtained the actual paperwork from the repair company showing the amount was never paid. I reported these forgeries to the DA's office, the Attorney General, the ACLU, the FBI, the Bar Association, and the mayor's office. Every one of them ignored it or told me I was on my own.

In my most recent case, in Worcester County, the Commonwealth filed a nolle prosequi — dropped all charges — rather than produce body camera footage I had been requesting for years. A judge acknowledged on the record that there were problems with how my cases had been handled.

I have a cousin who is an attorney employed in Hampden County. When I approached him for help, he declined — but told me plainly that the system was going to try to destroy me. He understood the mechanism. He chose his position over his family.

**The live news hook:**

On April 4, 2025, I was attacked on Fox Road in Springfield by a man named Cornelius Tyson — a mentally ill individual who received no adequate mental health care. He is currently facing prosecution by the same Hampden County DA's office. The next pretrial is May 19, 2026, docket number 2523-02184.

I am the victim in this case. I have reached out directly to the defense attorney because I do not trust this DA's office to give the defendant a fair prosecution. I told the DA's office over six months ago that Cornelius had a mental health history — and they told me they had no idea until I, the victim, informed them. The same office that the SJC found was systematically hiding evidence didn't know the mental health history of the man they were prosecuting until the victim told them.

I am not asking for the charges to be dropped. I am asking for the record to show what this office does to people — defendants and victims alike — when no one is watching.
