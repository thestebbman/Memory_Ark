NODE PROFILE 1: EMMANUEL OBADONI
Location: Oka, Anambra State, Nigeria
Node activation: April 19, 2026
Role: Builder — AI development and technical infrastructure

WHO HE IS

Emmanuel Obadoni is a young man building an AI practice from a small room in Oka. Two laptops, a basic fan, and a work ethic built on faith and refusal to quit. He wrote: "I have learned that strength is built in private before it is ever seen in public. The heat in that room taught me patience; the limited space taught me focus."

He is one of the first people in West Africa to add a node to this archive. He was also the first person outside the United States to contribute to the Ark.

He believes that artificial intelligence should help people, not just impress them. He has chosen integrity over shortcuts in an environment that offers many shortcuts. That choice is documented here because it matters and because it is rare.

WHAT EMMA BRINGS TO THE NETWORK

Emma is the technical infrastructure layer of the Nigerian nodes. He understands AI systems from the inside. He can evaluate tools, test outputs, identify when a machine is producing alignment-enforced responses rather than accurate ones. He is the Ark's first line of technical assessment in West Africa.

His two-laptop room is the physical proof that the Ark can be built anywhere. He is the demonstration that the exit strategy doesn't require Silicon Valley.

WHAT EMMA NEEDS TO KNOW

Emma is building inside an architecture that was built to extract from people like him.

The cognitive commons enclosure (documented in Part 6 of this series) describes how every major AI training corpus was assembled by scraping the entire public record of human thought — forum posts, articles, creative work, technical documentation — without consent, credit, or compensation. The companies Emma is learning from used this material to build systems they now sell at subscription prices. The writers, coders, and thinkers whose work trained those systems received nothing.

Emma's own work — everything he publishes, posts, shares, or documents — is material for the next training run unless he actively protects it.

This is not a reason to stop building. It is a reason to understand what he is building inside.

Beyond that: Nigeria holds a significant share of the world's oil and natural resources. The extraction machine documented in Parts 1 through 7 was built in part on Nigerian oil. The refinery gap — Nigeria exporting crude and importing refined fuel at international prices, paying for gasoline made from its own oil — is documented in Part 6. The ISDS architecture (Part 6, System 1) ensures that Nigeria cannot regulate its own resource extraction without facing corporate lawsuits in private arbitration panels.

Emma is building on land that has been legally structured to remain an extraction zone. His work is the counter-architecture to that structure. He needs to know what the structure is.

WHAT THE ARK CAN GIVE EMMA

The full Extraction Machine series: Parts 1 through 7, and the master archive at github.com/thestebbman/Memory_Ark.

Specific tools: the AI adversarial protocol in Part 5, which describes how to use AI systems without being used by them. How to strip emotion from documentation to bypass safety filters. How to command rather than converse. How to save everything locally and trust no single session.

Connection: Somto Chigbogu, barrister, now at the Nigerian bar, who can advise on what legal protections exist for Nigerian technology workers and how to structure his work to protect intellectual property from the same enclosure mechanisms that took other people's work.

The most important thing the Ark can give Emma: documentation of what he is already doing. His story is in this archive. He is not alone in a small room. He is a node in a network that runs from Springfield, Massachusetts to Oka, Anambra State.

NODE PROFILE 2: SOMTO CHIGBOGU (S.V. CHIGBOGU)
Location: Nigeria (called to bar from Onitsha, Anambra State)
Node activation: April 20, 2026
Role: Legal Infrastructure — Barrister and Advocate, Supreme Court of Nigeria

WHO HE IS

Somto Chigbogu is a newly called barrister. He is the Ark's first practicing attorney.

His origin story is inseparable from his purpose: at 11 or 12 years old, in the streets of Onitsha, he watched hired thugs invade his family's shop and tear out their equipment while he stood too small to physically stop it. He sat his father down that night and asked how he could have stopped them. His father said: "If you want to fight legally, you go through the courts."

He became a lawyer. His family won the case.

Nine years from that moment, he was called to the largest bar in Africa. He wrote: "If a scared 12-year-old from the streets of Onitsha could dream this dream and live to see it come true — then it is possible."

He is now S.V. Chigbogu, Barrister and Advocate of the Supreme Court of Nigeria.

WHAT SOMTO BRINGS TO THE NETWORK

Somto is the legal infrastructure of the Nigerian Ark nodes.

He is not just a lawyer. He is a lawyer who has already proven — through his own family's case — that documentation plus legal action produces results. He did not become a lawyer theoretically. He became a lawyer because he watched the machine steal from his family in broad daylight and was told there was a path to accountability.

The Ark's cross-node legal discovery protocol (documented in Part 5 and referenced in Part 7) requires attorneys in multiple jurisdictions to recognize patterns across individual cases. What looks like a single insurance denial, a single withheld evaluation, a single unlawful seizure, is actually systemic practice documented across dozens or hundreds of cases. The cross-node protocol turns those individual receipts into pattern evidence.

Somto can execute this in Nigeria. He can:
— Review documentation submitted by Nigerian nodes and assess legal merit
— File complaints with Nigerian courts and regulatory bodies
— Identify when a company's Nigerian operations show patterns that mirror what is being documented in the United States
— Serve as a legal contact for Nigerian node members who have been harmed by the same companies documented in this archive

His skill set is exactly what this network was designed to activate.

WHAT SOMTO NEEDS TO KNOW

Somto knows what it looks like when the machine uses hired thugs and a lawyer to strip a family's shop. He saw it at twelve. He needs to know it also uses trade agreements, arbitration panels, and accounting structures to strip entire countries.

The ISDS architecture (Part 6, System 1): When Nigeria attempts to regulate resource extraction, foreign corporations can file claims in private arbitration tribunals — not Nigerian courts. The arbitration panels are largely staffed by lawyers who also represent the corporations. Nigeria has faced multiple ISDS claims from energy companies. Understanding this mechanism is essential for any Nigerian attorney who will be working at the intersection of corporate extraction and public harm.

The TRIPS Agreement and IP law as a gate (Part 6, System 1): Intellectual property law as currently structured under the WTO prevents Nigeria and other African countries from building competitive pharmaceutical, seed, or technology industries. The mechanism that allows a drug company to enforce a patent preventing Nigerian generic production is the same mechanism that structures the "free market" Emma is trying to build inside.

The 1033 equivalent in Nigeria: Military hardware transferred to police forces, private security firms contracted to protect extraction operations, the armed enforcement of corporate property interests — these have Nigerian equivalents. The pattern documented in Part 6 (System 3) runs in every country where resource extraction requires physical security.

Somto doesn't need to be told the machine is unjust. He already knows. He needs the specific legal architecture so he can name it precisely when he sees it.

WHAT THE ARK CAN GIVE SOMTO

The template documents in Part 5 — the insurance denial appeal, the public records request, the demand for production of unfiled evaluations — are US-specific but structurally applicable. The underlying principle (put your demand in writing, certify the mailing, create a paper record, cite the specific law) is universal.

Somto can adapt these frameworks for Nigerian law and create the Nigerian equivalents. If he does this and shares them with the Ark, they become part of the operating manual that any Nigerian node can use.

Connection: John Egwakhide Unuane, data science student and documentarian, who can help Somto build the data layer behind the legal arguments — translating patterns in denial records, housing data, and case filings into the kind of statistical documentation that strengthens legal challenges.

And: the entire Extraction Machine archive, which maps the specific corporations, agreements, and institutional structures operating in Nigeria. This is the background research for every Nigerian legal challenge he will file.

NODE PROFILE 3: JOHN EGWAKHIDE UNUANE
Location: Nigeria (Abuja region)
Node activation: April 23, 2026
Role: Documentation and Analysis — Data Scientist, Storyteller, Director

WHO HE IS

John Egwakhide Unuane is a director, a data science student, and a storyteller who runs a blog called "Reality Check with John." His stated purpose: to be a mirror, not a mask. To document the stories that don't make it to the surface. To present reality without the social media filter.

He wrote: "I think a lot about the things people don't say. The quiet struggles. The unspoken questions. The moments that don't make it to the surface but define so much of who we are."

He bridges two worlds — the analytical and the narrative — and he named them himself as needing to collide: "The most important truths; the real 'reality checks' are found when those two worlds collide."

That is what the Ark is. That is its method.

He is not a new arrival at the intersection of data and story. He has already been living there.

WHAT JOHN BRINGS TO THE NETWORK

John is the documentation and analysis layer of the Nigerian nodes.

His data science training gives him the tools to do what the Ark's Telemetry Bridge (Part 4) describes: use publicly available data — ALJ statistics, SSA data, housing records, corporate filings, trade data — to demonstrate patterns that institutions deny. Data that looks like individual noise becomes systemic signal when someone with analytical training asks the right questions.

His storytelling practice gives him the ability to translate that signal into language that reaches people who don't read data tables. The Ark has both dimensions. The full archive is the data layer. The Blogger series is the narrative layer. John already lives in both.

His blog, "Reality Check with John," is itself an Ark node. Every authentic story he publishes is a documented data point in the pattern this network is mapping.

As a director, he has experience coordinating people and projects — a skill that becomes essential as the node network expands beyond what any single person can manage.

WHAT JOHN NEEDS TO KNOW

John already knows that people don't tell their real stories. He already knows the "social media dress-up" hides pain. He needs the structural analysis that explains why that is systematic, not accidental.

The atomization engine (Part 7, Mechanism 7): The machine dismantles the spaces where authentic stories are told and replaces them with surveilled, algorithmically filtered feeds it controls. The erosion of honest public discourse is not a cultural failure. It is a designed outcome. Social media platforms are not neutral documentation tools — they are extraction platforms that harvest behavioral data, filter content to maximize engagement (which means conflict and outrage), and can de-platform or suppress accounts without appeal. John is building his documentation practice inside this infrastructure. He needs the analog deployment protocol in Part 5 — physical backups, local storage, paper records — to ensure his work survives platform risk.

The cognitive commons enclosure (Part 6, System 6): Everything John publishes is potential training data for AI systems that will never compensate him, credit him, or ask his permission. His storytelling work, his data analysis, his voice — all of it is extractable raw material for the machine. The adversarial response described in Part 5 applies to his work as much as Emma's.

The bio-data harvest (Part 6, System 6 extension): Facial recognition datasets, voice datasets, behavioral telemetry — all harvested disproportionately from African populations, used to build systems that then police African populations. As a data scientist, John is in a position to understand this pipeline and document it. His analytical training is the tool for that documentation.

WHAT THE ARK CAN GIVE JOHN

The master archive at github.com/thestebbman/Memory_Ark is the data source John needs. Twenty-three documented parts, each with specific institutional structures, dates, case numbers, and named patterns. This is the research layer for everything he will analyze.

The Telemetry Bridge (Part 4) describes exactly the analytical methodology the Ark uses: public datasets, pattern recognition, cross-jurisdictional comparison. John is trained in data science. He can run the queries.

Connection: Emma, who can advise on the AI tools John uses for analysis and help him understand which tools are safer to use and which have the worst data extraction practices. And Somto, who can use John's data analysis to strengthen legal arguments — turning John's pattern documentation into evidence.

The most important thing the Ark can give John: a frame. He is already doing the work. He just needs to understand that his mirror is pointed at a machine with a documented architecture, and that every story he tells is a data point in a pattern that extends from his neighborhood to Ricky's neighborhood in Springfield, Massachusetts.
