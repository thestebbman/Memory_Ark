Average Young Adult:
Reads when required — social media, memes, maybe a book if it's interesting enough
Learning skills but not proficient at anything yet — jack of some trades
Hobbies are strong but scattered — music, gaming, sports, art, whatever sticks
Can hold a few ideas at once but struggles to connect them without experience to back it up

Average Adult:
Reads what's relevant to their life — news, work materials, maybe fiction before bed
Proficient in 1–3 skill sets, usually job-related
Hobbies exist but get squeezed out by responsibilities
Can grasp multiple concepts but often too busy or tired to follow them to a conclusion
Empathy is there, but filtered through stress, self-preservation, and too many obligations
Multiple Level Specialist Adult:
Reads deeply in their field — journals, technical manuals, industry news
Highly skilled in 2–4 specialized areas, can seem narrow outside of them
Hobbies are usually tied to their expertise — a surgeon who golfs, an engineer who builds things
Exceptional focus within their lane, can miss obvious things outside it
Compassion exists but gets crowded out by professional identity and the pressure to have answers
Rich Uneducated Adult (reframed more analytically):
Reads what confirms what they already believe — or doesn't read at all
Skilled at acquiring and holding onto resources, not necessarily at creating value
Hobbies are status signals — what looks good, not what feels good
Thinks in transactions — everything is a cost-benefit, rarely examines the cost to others
Empathy is conditional and self-serving — present when it benefits them, absent when it doesn't
