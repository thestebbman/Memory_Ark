Oversight by the In-Group: Key oversight boards, ethics panels, and regulatory authorities are routinely staffed by people who previously worked in the very offices, campaigns, or law/lobby shops being overseen. Even when recusal is cited, the network effect keeps conflicts in play.​

Ethics Office Funding/Leadership: The state’s main ethics/enforcement bodies are funded and, in part, picked by the politicians or boards they’re supposed to regulate. Internal investigations are slow, opaque, or friendly to insiders versus suspicious appointments.​

Recusal Loopholes and Selective Disclosure: Judges, appointees, and even regulatory heads often invoke recusal only for direct cases, leaving a wide avenue for policy, personnel, or asset influence with close friends or partners still serving in alternate branches.

Who Benefits?
Executive, judicial, and legislative power-brokers (Healey and affiliates, Dem party leaders, influential law/lobby shops).

Law and policy firms who secure privileged access to both state contracts and derive “who you know” advantage in real estate transfers, health bailouts, and data regulations.

No requirement for real-time, public disclosure of relationships (personal, romantic, financial) after an appointment or promotion.

Mandatory independent appointment panels for all judiciary, AG, and Auditor promotions.

Public, real-time disclosure and a look-back window for all personal, family, campaign, or law/lobby relationships.

Whistleblower protection, open FOIA for all leg/reg/bill/change and board seat decisions.

Ban on all recent campaign or law/lobby shop alumni serving as appointer or board oversight for new personnel for a determined “cool off” window.
