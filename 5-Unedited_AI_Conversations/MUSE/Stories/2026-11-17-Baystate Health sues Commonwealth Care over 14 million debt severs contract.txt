Baystate Health sues Commonwealth Care over $14 million debt, severs contract
Published: Nov. 17, 2026, 6:26 p.m.
Baystate Health in Springfield

By Stephanie Barry | sbarry@repub.com
SPRINGFIELD — Baystate Health on Tuesday announced that they will sever their contract with a large insurance provider for low-income and disabled members over an alleged $14 million debt.

Commonwealth Care Alliance, commonly known as CCA, provides healthcare coverage for about 50,000 Massachusetts residents, according to its website.

The regional healthcare giant posted the announcement to its website hours after The Republican emailed to inquire about an online posting about the matter.

Baystate argues CCA “improperly recouped” payments and violated the terms of their agreements for care that had already been delivered to patients.

“Baystate cannot continue to contract with an insurer that does not pay for the care we provide for its members in accordance with the terms of the agreement in place,” the announcement reads.



The contract is set to expire on Dec. 31.

“With nearly $14 million owed for care already delivered, these are the resources that support the services families in our communities depend on. As a safety-net healthcare provider, we have a responsibility to protect those resources and ensure we can continue to meet the healthcare needs of our region,” it continues.

A spokesperson for the Ohio-based company that acquired CCA in 2025, acknowledged failed negotiations with the local network.

“We have been working in good faith to resolve outstanding issues with Baystate Health while negotiating a renewed contract. Despite these efforts, Baystate has communicated its intent to leave the provider network,” said Vicki McDonald, spokesperson for CareSource.


“We recognize the concern this creates for members, and while we continue working toward an agreement, we have started notifying them that Baystate Health, including its newly acquired Mercy Medical Center, could leave our network on January 1, 2027,” she added.

The development comes just weeks after Baystate acquired the former Mercy Medical Center for $292.7 million. Staff later tore down religious icons throughout the Catholic hospital and are creating some level of uncertainty for patients with changes in technology and staffing.

“The expiration of these contracts is coincidental and unrelated to the integration of Mercy Medical Center and its affiliated practices into Baystate Health,” the announcement says.

McDonald said the company has recovered “past overpayments” and improved payment practices going forward, suggesting that is where the financial dispute lies.

“Financial disputes between healthcare organizations should not come at the expense of the patients who depend on their care. We remain ready to reach a reasonable agreement that protects our members’ access to care while responsibly managing the fund,” she said.

Alternative health plan options for patients currently enrolled in CCA that include Baystate Health providers in their networks include Fallon NaviCare for senior care options, Tufts Public Plan One Care and Molina Health Senior Whole Health.

If patients would prefer to stick with CCA, they can contact the company and figure it out, the statement essentially says.
