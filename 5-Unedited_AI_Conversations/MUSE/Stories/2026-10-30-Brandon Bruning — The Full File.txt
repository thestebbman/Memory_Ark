3. THE COOKING STORY - THE HELP EXISTS AND WAS NEVER OFFERED
Brandon's claim, which Ricky asked us to test on October 30,
2026: Massachusetts has programs that could help him cook
safely, and the funding for them exists. Verdict of the
check: he is right. (Full check with sources:
brandon-cooking-tech-check-20261030.txt.)

- Massachusetts DDS runs the Technology Forward Initiative.
Its Supportive Technology program = Assistive Technology
(AT) plus Remote Supports & Monitoring. The route is a
service-coordinator referral, then an AT assessment, then
funded devices and funded training. DDS materials name
stove sensors by name. There are 14 AT providers and 10
remote-supports providers in the program.
- SafeInHome, a DDS-listed remote-supports provider, markets
a package called "Kitchen Safety": remote staff check-ins
during kitchen activities, sensors that detect smoke and
heat or automatically shut off a stove, staff available
24/7 by tablet. Their own success-story page is about a
client named Brandon - a different man, same first name,
same need - who cooks with these supports.
- SimplyHome, also DDS-listed: a stove sensor that gives the
person verbal cues ("turn off the stove") and sends
real-time alerts to caregivers - built, in other words,
for getting sidetracked.
- The step-by-step apps Ricky asked for exist: Cognitopia's
Goal Guide (used in real cooking classes for students with
cognitive disabilities - a step per screen, a video per
step, a teacher able to watch progress remotely) and
Routines app; CreateAbility's MeMinder (video modeling and
step sequencing, designed to fade as skill grows);
AbleLink Visual Impact; CanPlan.
- The literal camera-coach - an AI that watches and catches
skipped steps - exists as research (CHEF-VL, Washington
University in St. Louis, 2025; and UMass Lowell inside the
NSF's AI-CARING institute, working on AI to help people
"prepare their meals safely").
- The rot underneath: Easterseals Massachusetts' AT program
- the state's only comprehensive AT contract - reports its
capacity down 68 percent over five years on funding cuts.

So: the state funds stove shutoffs, remote kitchen check-ins,
and step-following cooking apps, through an assessment
Brandon's own service coordinator can refer him for. In
seven years of total ban - and in the ten months since the
January "cooking class" that taught him to cut potatoes and
watch the cooking channel - nobody offered him any of it.

THE ASK, in one line: a DDS AT assessment for Brandon
Bruning, and a written explanation of why seven years passed
without one.
