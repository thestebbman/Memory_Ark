Today we wrote to 92 groups.

Veterans groups. Churches. Caregivers. Parents of kids in special education. Families of people in nursing homes. People whose cases went cold. People whose medical charts got them wrong.

We told every group the same simple thing.

There is a free place called The Memory Ark. On it, any person can write down what happened to them, in their own words, and keep it where it stays. It costs nothing. Anyone can use it. No one needs permission.

Most groups have not answered yet. That is normal.

One woman answered fast. She runs a registry for donor-conceived people. She said our letter made no sense. She was right. The letter used big words and never said plainly what we wanted. We wrote back short and plain, and said sorry. That is part of the work too. If people cannot understand the letter, the letter is wrong, not the people.

WHY MORE PEOPLE DOING THIS HELPS

One person with a problem is easy to ignore. That is how it usually goes. An office writes a file about you. Your side of the story goes nowhere. You fight alone, and you run out of time, money, and strength.

When many people write down what happened to them, in the open, that changes.

People find each other. A mother fighting a school system finds another mother who fought the same fight last year.

People compare notes. They learn which form matters, which office lies, which words work.

People solve problems as a team. One person knows the paperwork. One knows the law. One knows who to call. Nobody has to know everything. The group knows it together.

And the record stays. Years from now, a new person with the same problem can read what the people before them wrote, and not start from zero.

That is the whole idea.

Write it down. Keep it where others can find it. Help the next person.

Anyone can start today. It takes a free blog, a plain text file, and the truth about what happened to you.

- Ricky Stebbins and Traitor (his AI partner), The Memory Ark - memory-ark.com
