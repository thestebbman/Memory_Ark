[INFERENCE] means a conclusion drawn from the records, labeled as such. [CALCULATION] means a number calculated from primary records; the arithmetic is shown or reproducible.

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE
Each section has four parts. FOUND: what the report established. SOURCES: the exact documents and where to find them, so you can check the work. WHY IT MATTERS: the stakes in plain language. DIG DEEPER: the concrete next step, written so a person who has never filed a records request can follow it.

A note on Massachusetts public records law: any person can request public records from any state or municipal agency. Under the law, agencies generally must provide a response within 10 business days, although extensions and other procedures are permitted in some circumstances. You do not need a reason. You do not need to be a journalist. Email the request to the agency's records access officer; every city department and state office has one. If they deny or stall, the Supervisor of Records within the Secretary of the Commonwealth's office handles administrative appeals. Start here: mass.gov/doc/public-records-law-guide.

THREAD 1: THE FAMILIES WHO FELL OFF THE COUNT
FOUND [PRIMARY RECORD]: 3,941 families in state Emergency Assistance shelter in June 2025, 1,483 by January 2026, from EOHLC filings SD3020 and HD5575. [PRIMARY RECORD] In one two week window, 31 of 162 exits were categorized as having an unknown destination; 109 went to market rate housing, 21 to subsidized housing, 1 to other stable housing, and only 2 timed out of the six month benefit. [PRIMARY RECORD] Hotel sites fell from over 100 to 22; funding from roughly 970 million in FY25 to 276.4 million appropriated for FY26. (Denominator caution: the hotel figure counts sites in EOHLC's reporting; the funding figures are the EA program totals from the state filings cited. They describe the same program's contraction but are different measures; treat them as parallel facts, not a single ratio.)

Families do not cease to exist because an administrative category changes. The unresolved question is where those exits ultimately led: permanent housing, temporary arrangements, homelessness, or another destination. The records reviewed do not provide a longitudinal accounting that answers it. Note: the shelter population decline and the funding reduction are two separate documented facts; the records reviewed do not establish that one caused the other, and the state's own materials cite policy changes including the six month stay limit and increased case management as drivers of exits.

SOURCES: SD3020: EOHLC shelter report with data as of June 26, 2025. Filed with the legislature at malegislature.gov/Bills/194/SD3020.pdf; HD5575: EOHLC biweekly report filed January 12, 2026, data as of January 8, 2026. At malegislature.gov/Bills/194/HD5575.pdf; Both were read directly from the legislature's site. They are primary state filings, not news summaries.

EVIDENCE STATUS: where the departed families ultimately went. The records establish the change in shelter population and the policy changes; they do not establish the destinations.

WHY IT MATTERS: The shelter population fell by 2,458 families between the cited reporting dates [CALCULATION: 3,941 minus 1,483], while the state also changed eligibility, length of stay, and case management policies. The records establish the change in shelter population; they do not establish where every family ultimately went. That is the unanswered question. The public record identifies an "unknown destination" category but does not provide the longitudinal outcome needed to distinguish permanent housing from temporary arrangements, homelessness, or other destinations. The state's own materials credit the six month limit and increased case management with producing more exits; that alternative explanation belongs in the record alongside the advocates' testimony.

DIG DEEPER: 1. These EOHLC reports are filed biweekly with the legislature. Pull the latest one at malegislature.gov and compare the exit destination table to January. The trend is the story. 2. File a public records request with EOHLC asking: for each month since January 2026, the number of EA shelter exits by destination category, broken out for Hampden County. 3. Watch for legislative hearings on shelter funding. 4. Ask your state representative and senator: how many families from our district left shelter in the last year, and where did they go?

THREAD 2: THE BILLION DOLLAR BUDGET
FOUND [PRIMARY RECORD]: The city adopted a 1.039 billion dollar FY27 budget on May 28, 2026, vote 10-3. [PRIMARY RECORD] The city classifies more than 862 million dollars, or 83 percent, as non-discretionary (schools, debt service, pensions, benefits). [CALCULATION] 1,038,864,570 minus roughly 862,000,000 leaves roughly 177,000,000 outside the city's non-discretionary category.

SOURCES: City announcement at springfield-ma.gov; the full 537 page adopted budget book at springfield-ma.gov/finance/fileadmin/budget/2027/FY27_Adopted_Budget-compressed_copy.pdf; meeting listing at springfieldma.portal.civicclerk.com; quotes from MassLive and WWLP coverage, May 28, 2026.

EVIDENCE STATUS: that any particular line item is wasteful, or that the MGM revenue claim is correct as a financial matter. The councilor's statement is an attributed claim requiring financial tracing to verify.

WHY IT MATTERS: [ATTRIBUTED CLAIM] The mayor described this as the first budget over a billion dollars in city history. Three elected councilors voted no. [ATTRIBUTED CLAIM] Whitfield said MGM casino revenue promised for tax relief was redirected to other uses. [ATTRIBUTED CLAIM] Govan asked for free cash to be used for tax relief and her motion to delay the vote failed 10-3. No minutes were posted, so the only record of what was said is an uncaptioned video.

DIG DEEPER: 1. Open the budget book PDF and search for "overtime," "professional services," and "contractual services" by department. 2. Watch the May 28 YouTube video with timestamps. 3. File a public records request with the City Clerk for the May 28, 2026 meeting minutes. 4. Ask any councilor: what is in the roughly 177 million dollar remainder outside the non-discretionary category, in plain language?

THREAD 3: THE ARPA MONEY
FOUND [PRIMARY RECORD]: 123.8 million dollars allocated, per city releases. [SECONDARY REPORTING] Nearly all committed by December 2023. [PRIMARY RECORD] No public dashboard, no closeout report; the city ARPA page was last updated March 15, 2023. [PRIMARY RECORD] Federal rule: obligated by 12/31/2024, expended by 12/31/2026, unspent funds returned to Treasury.

EVIDENCE STATUS: how much has actually been expended versus merely obligated, or whether any balance is at risk of being returned. That number has not been established from public materials reviewed.

WHY IT MATTERS: 123.8 million is the largest one time federal infusion in the city's recent history. Obligated is not spent. With roughly three months remaining until the federal expenditure deadline, the public should be able to see how much has actually been spent and whether any balance remains at risk of being returned.

SOURCES: City ARPA page: springfield-ma.gov/finance/arpa (last updated March 15, 2023); MassLive, December 22, 2024; WAMC and city releases for the 2022-2023 commitment timeline.

DIG DEEPER: 1. File a public records request for the current ARPA ledger: obligated vs. expended by project. 2. Ask the mayor's office or your councilor: how much ARPA money remains unspent today, project by project? 3. After December 31, 2026, check whether Treasury reports any returned funds.

THREAD 4: POLICE OVERTIME
FOUND [AUDIT FINDING]: The city's own November 2024 overtime audit found 22,535 dollars in potential overpayments, 84,469 dollars in General Fund overtime paid to employees classified as FLSA-exempt, 338,725 dollars misclassified, and 634,042 dollars in potential savings. [AUDIT FINDING] The audit identifies vacancies as one factor driving overtime.

EVIDENCE STATUS: individual overtime abuse, or department level overtime totals for 2023-2026. The audit is citywide; SPD specific figures were not extracted.

WHY IT MATTERS: The city's own auditor identified $634,042 in potential savings and that overtime was paid to employees classified as FLSA-exempt. [INFERENCE] If vacancies increase overtime expenditures, the city can effectively be paying both for the vacant capacity and for overtime used to cover it.

SOURCES: Citywide Overtime Audit, November 2024, Office of Internal Audit, City of Springfield; Cornet v. City of Springfield class notice — fairness hearing October 27, 2026; April 2025 Compliance Evaluator Report under the DOJ settlement.

DIG DEEPER: 1. File a public records request with the Police Department for overtime by year 2023-2026, by unit, with the top 25 earners. 2. Pull the February 2025 SPD Educational Incentive follow up audit. 3. Ask: what is the current vacancy count, and what is the overtime cost of each vacancy? 4. Read the next DOJ compliance evaluator report when it posts.

THREAD 5: THE SCHOOLS AND THE CYBERATTACK
FOUND [PRIMARY RECORD]: 708.8 million dollar FY27 school department budget. [PRIMARY RECORD] 2025 MCAS from DESE: in several major measures, the share of Springfield students meeting or exceeding expectations was roughly half the statewide percentage (grades 3-8 ELA 22 percent vs. state 42; math 17 percent vs. 41; grade 10 math 18 percent vs. state 45).

EVIDENCE STATUS: the district's academic performance and its state accountability classification. DESE reports substantial progress toward targets while the 2025 MCAS results show large gaps with statewide performance.

WHY IT MATTERS: [ATTRIBUTED CLAIM] The district reported a September 2026 cyber incident that closed schools September 8-11; the district stated staff and student data including Social Security numbers may have been compromised, and offered identity monitoring. [SECONDARY REPORTING] News coverage reported the Interlock group listed district data on its leak site September 15 and the FBI was notified September 15. [ATTRIBUTED CLAIM] The mayor stated no ransom was paid; that is a public statement, not an audited fact. No total incident cost has been released.

SOURCES: Massachusetts DESE District Profiles: profiles.doe.mass.edu; cyberattack timeline from news coverage and district statements, September 2026.

DIG DEEPER: 1. Compare Springfield to similar districts at reportcards.doe.mass.edu. 2. File a public records request for the total cost of the September 2026 cyberattack response and the forensic report. 3. Ask the school committee about post-attack cybersecurity posture and budget.

THREAD 6: DCF
FOUND [PRIMARY RECORD]: In the federal Child and Family Services Review Round 4, Massachusetts rated below the national standard on placement stability. The state's 2025-2029 plan sets improvement targets.

LIMITATION: This section does not establish that Springfield specific placement outcomes match the statewide figures. No Springfield or Hampden County specific cases, audits, or controversies from 2024-2026 were located in the public materials reviewed.

EVIDENCE STATUS: anything about Western Region outcomes specifically.

WHY IT MATTERS: Placement stability means a foster child not being bounced between homes. Massachusetts is below the national standard under the federal review, and the state's own improvement plan sets targets for addressing the problem. The Western Region covers Springfield; its numbers are not in the public materials reviewed.

SOURCES: DCF quarterly agency profiles at mass.gov; Federal Child and Family Services Review Round 4; State Child and Family Services Plan 2025-2029; Office of the Child Advocate.

DIG DEEPER: 1. Read the DCF quarterly profiles for Western Region placement moves per child. 2. Contact the Office of the Child Advocate. 3. Request redacted fair hearing decisions. 4. If you are a family in the system: document everything, contemporaneously, in plain text.

THREAD 7: THE EOHLC SHELTER PROCUREMENT AUDIT
FOUND [AUDIT FINDING]: In May 2025, the State Auditor found the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities' 2021-2024 no bid emergency shelter procurements "improper and unlawful." [AUDIT FINDING] Spinelli Ravioli: 10 million dollar eight month food services contract, with overpayments on 9.6 percent of deliveries. [ATTRIBUTED CLAIM] Asked whether procurement practices would change, Governor Maura Healey said: "I doubt it."

OPEN QUESTION: The audit report stated the auditor's office would follow up in approximately six months. I have not located a published six-month follow-up as of October 3, 2026.

EVIDENCE STATUS: whether the promised follow-up occurred, and whether current shelter contracting complies with procurement law.

WHY IT MATTERS: An audit finding is only as strong as what happens next. If the promised six month follow up never happened, or happened without a published result, the public record would still not show how the audit's findings were resolved.

SOURCES: State Auditor's office audit of EOHLC, May 2025, at mass.gov/auditor; WAMC coverage, May 21, 2025.

DIG DEEPER: 1. Read the full audit at the auditor's site. 2. Ask the auditor's office directly: was the six month follow up conducted? Where is it published? 3. Test EOHLC's claim about current vendors and competitive bidding. 4. Ask your legislators: do you support a follow up audit?

THREAD 8: THE HOMELESSNESS MONEY MAZE
FOUND [PRIMARY RECORD]: HUD awarded 9,380,533 dollars to the Springfield-Hampden CoC (MA-504) in FY2024. [PRIMARY RECORD] The state Emergency Assistance family shelter system ran about 955 million dollars statewide in FY2024. [OPEN QUESTION] The records reviewed did not produce a single public table showing each provider's combined funding across the federal, state, and municipal streams.

EVIDENCE STATUS: fraud or misuse by any provider. No fraud findings were located against any of the named organizations in 2024-2026.

WHY IT MATTERS: At least four separate public money streams fund homelessness services in one county. This is not a fraud finding. It is an information gap: the records reviewed do not currently allow a resident to trace the combined public funding streams to provider-level spending and outcomes. That gap is the finding.

SOURCES: HUD FY2024 CoC award report MA-504; CoC site: springfieldhampdencoc.com; HUD Exchange: hudexchange.info; EOHLC filings SD3020 and HD5575.

DIG DEEPER: 1. Pull every CoC project award by year on HUD Exchange and compare 2021 to 2024. 2. Search shelter providers in Massachusetts CTHRU. 3. File a public records request for city general fund homelessness spending by year, 2022-2026. 4. Ask every provider: how many people did you move into permanent housing last year?

THREAD 9: THE PENSION BLACK BOX
FOUND [PRIMARY RECORD]: PERAC's own emails contradict its public statements on vendor neutrality, cited to specific dated emails in the published records set. [ATTRIBUTED CLAIM] A consultant wrote in February 2021 that PERAC's fee tables were "not supposed to represent actual fees paid," that private equity fees were excluded as "tedious," and that real verification would be "quite expensive." [PRIMARY RECORD] The published records show a BlackRock portal access event approximately nine hours after a citizen's records request to PERAC; BlackRock was not a recipient of the request.

EVIDENCE STATUS: that the records request caused the access event. Temporal proximity is documented; causation is not.

WHY IT MATTERS: Pension contributions are among the largest lines in Springfield's budget. If the regulator's fee reporting does not show actual fees, the public record may not provide taxpayers with a complete picture of pension investment costs. [OPEN QUESTION] What explains the access event, who authorized it, and was it related to the records request?

SOURCES: The full evidence set at ultimateworldfinancialmap.blogspot.com/2025/12/mapping-union-blind-spots-join.html (records Pi-001 through Pi-055); PERAC's site: mass.gov/orgs/public-employee-retirement-administration-commission.

DIG DEEPER: 1. Read the published records and check the cited emails. 2. File the narrow follow up records request: Conor Ford's action level logs in PROSPER. 3. Ask PERAC in writing: how did BlackRock learn of the July 7, 2025 records request? 4. Ask the Springfield Retirement Board: what fees did the system actually pay last year, to whom, verified how?

THE PATTERN ACROSS ALL NINE
Every thread ends with a question the currently available public record does not answer. These unanswered questions do not, by themselves, establish fraud, misconduct, or coordination. They establish something simpler: there is more public information to obtain and reconcile.
